# Meeting-to-Execution Copilot

## 1. Product context

Teams make decisions in meetings, but the resulting action items and unresolved questions can be scattered across notes, chat messages, and project-management tools.

A meeting summary may look complete while failing to capture who owns a task, whether a decision was actually made, or what still needs clarification.

The Meeting-to-Execution Copilot focuses on making meeting outcomes actionable and auditable.

## 2. Problem statement

Teams need a reliable way to convert meeting transcripts into explicit actions, owners, deadlines, decisions, and open questions. Generic summaries often fail to preserve accountability and ambiguity.

## 3. Target users

**Primary user:** Product managers coordinating work across design, engineering, and business teams.

**Secondary users:** Project managers, team leads, and startup founders.

## 4. Core hypothesis

If meeting outcomes are converted into structured action items with evidence from the transcript and clear uncertainty flags, teams will be able to follow through more reliably.

## 5. Proposed MVP

- Paste or upload a meeting transcript.
- Extract action items and decisions.
- Identify explicitly assigned owners and deadlines.
- Separate confirmed decisions from proposals and unresolved questions.
- Link each extracted item to its source passage.
- Flag missing owners, unclear commitments, and ambiguous dates.
- Allow users to edit and approve the output.
- Export approved actions as a checklist or CSV.

Start with transcript text. Recording meetings, real-time transcription, and multiple integrations can come later.

## 6. Product differentiation

The product should not invent ownership or turn suggestions into commitments.

For example, if a participant says, "We should investigate this next week," the system should not automatically assign a named owner or convert the statement into a confirmed deadline.

Every important extracted item should be reviewable against the original transcript.

## 7. PM decisions to demonstrate

- What qualifies as an action item rather than a general discussion point?
- How should the system represent uncertainty?
- When should it ask the user for clarification?
- What evidence is required before an action can be exported?
- How much manual review is acceptable?
- Which integrations are justified by actual user demand?

## 8. Success metrics

- Precision and recall of action-item extraction on a reviewed test set.
- Accuracy of owner and deadline extraction.
- Percentage of extracted actions accepted without correction.
- Rate of unsupported decisions or invented assignments.
- Time required to review and distribute meeting outcomes.
- Percentage of ambiguous items correctly flagged.

## 9. Initial validation

Use several sample transcripts with different meeting styles. Manually annotate the true decisions, action items, owners, deadlines, and unresolved questions.

Compare the system's output with the annotations and record both missed items and false positives.

## 10. Out of scope

- Autonomous assignment of work.
- Automatically sending commitments to colleagues.
- Full meeting recording infrastructure.
- Deep Jira, Slack, and Notion integrations in the first release.
- Employee performance scoring.

## 11. Portfolio outcome

Show the difference between a conventional meeting summary and an execution-ready output, with special attention to ambiguity handling, source traceability, and the trade-offs between automation and human approval.


- PM perspective: This project is especially useful for demonstrating workflow design and reliability. Its central question is whether it reduces follow-up work without creating incorrect commitments.
